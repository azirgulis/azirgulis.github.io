The map shows how much more a household’s net income rises when it has children under each country’s tax-and-benefit system (OECD TaxBEN). Countries differ widely.

These differences do not appear to drive fertility. Across Europe the cross-country link between child premiums and the total fertility rate is weak and unstable, and reform-based evaluations (Žirgulis, Čepėnas & Emmich, 2026) find the marginal incentive’s causal effect on births is a precise zero. The one robust behavioural response — Poland’s Family 500+ — appears in mothers’ employment, not in completed family size. Much of the apparent spread reflects the shared shape of tax schedules rather than policy choices that move births.
